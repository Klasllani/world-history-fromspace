Hormuz Bypass Map (2026)

This spatial process map models crude transport phenomena from Gas-Oil Separation Plants (GOSP) down to Single Point Mooring (SPM) offshore export terminals. Under hypothetical geopolitical closures or flow blockages within the Strait of Hormuz chokepoint, published hydrocarbon streams rely on two primary overland diversion pathways out of the western Persian Gulf basin: westward movement along the East-West Crude Oil Pipeline (Petroline) terminating at Yanbu on the Red Sea, and eastward transport through the Bab-Habshan-Fujairah pipeline (ADCOP) ending at Fujairah on the Gulf of Oman. Spatial nodes represent processing plants, oilfields, marine terminals, and maritime chokepoints sourced from public site registries, while linear features strictly delineate named mainlines. The embedded interactive interface integrates all underlying spatial and engineering data directly into each vector feature; selecting any individual node marker or pipeline segment opens an attribute popup detailing specific facility identifiers, trunkline names, nominal pipe diameters, operational nameplate capacities, and crude grade parameters. This visualization serves as a high-level transport architecture model and does not represent piping and instrumentation diagrams (P&ID), mechanical engineering detail drawings, control alarm setpoints, or infrastructure risk assets.

From a unit operations perspective, raw production moves sequentially through a rigorous upstream-to-downstream fluid handling hierarchy. The process commences at wellhead manifolds, feeding multi-stage Gas-Oil Separation Plants (GOSP) where flash vaporization depletes dissolved light hydrocarbons (C₁–C₄) from the bulk liquid stream. Liquid effluents undergo electrostatic desalting to drop out emulsified brine solids, followed by hydrogen sulfide (H₂S) stripping in stabilization columns to meet Reid Vapor Pressure (RVP) and sweetening specifications. Stabilized crude is subsequently pumped through high-pressure cross-country trunklines serviced by intermediate booster pump stations. Upon reaching coastal marine terminals (such as Yanbu or Fujairah), liquid crude is metered through custody transfer units into tank farms, then boosted via subsea pipelines to Single Point Mooring (SPM) calm buoys for deep-water Very Large Crude Carrier (VLCC) offloading.

In response to regional security vulnerabilities and potential blockades of the Strait of Hormuz, Saudi Aramco and regional operators maintain strategic operational flexibility. By expanding cross-peninsula transmission capacity on the 48/56-inch East-West Petroline corridor, utilizing Drag-Reducing Agents (DRA) to optimize hydraulic throughput, and expanding Red Sea loading berths, Saudi Arabia can divert up to 5.0 to 7.0 million barrels per day (MMbpd) of stabilized crude away from Gulf maritime bottlenecks directly into Red Sea shipping lanes.

Pipeline network topology and associated trunkline attributes—including nominal pipe diameters and operational nameplate volumetric capacities—are derived from the Global Energy Monitor (GEM) Global Oil Infrastructure Tracker (GOIT) dataset (June 2026 tabular metrics; July 21, 2026 spatial geometry release, licensed under CC BY 4.0). Mainline features extracted from the dataset include the East-West Crude Oil Pipeline, Shaybah-Abqaiq Oil Pipeline, Bab-Habshan-Fujairah Oil Pipeline, Abqaiq Plants-Qatif Junction Oil Pipelines, and Qatif Junction-Juaymah Terminal Oil Pipelines. Select filtering isolates liquid hydrocarbon lines currently operating under active crude oil service; dual Natural Gas Liquids (NGL) gathering systems and secondary field lines are excluded from this public layer.

Export slate classifications map directly to published petroleum assay standards. The baseline fluid classification assumes standard Arabian Light. Shaybah production maps to Arabian Super Light, Safaniya corresponds to Arabian Heavy, and Ras Laffan exports are modeled as Qatar Condensate. Thermophysical fluid conversions convert API gravity values to dimensionless specific gravity at standard reference conditions (60 °F / 15.56 °C):

SG = 141.5 / (°API + 131.5)
  • SG: Specific gravity of the crude hydrocarbon stream relative to pure water [dimensionless]
  • °API: American Petroleum Institute gravity standard expressing crude light-to-heavy density relationships [degrees API]

Liquid density is subsequently evaluated by scaling the specific gravity against the nominal reference density of pure water at standard conditions:

ρ = SG × ρ_water
  • ρ: Hydrocarbon liquid density [kg/m³]
  • SG: Calculated crude oil specific gravity [dimensionless]
  • ρ_water: Reference density of pure water [999 kg/m³]

Volumetric throughput rates are evaluated exclusively on network segments with confirmed public nameplate capacity data. Linear flow velocities are calculated by applying the fluid continuity equation to trunklines featuring uniform internal diameters and fixed capacities:

v = Q / A = (4 × Q) / (π × d²)
  • v: Mean inline axial fluid velocity [m/s]
  • Q: Volumetric throughput flow rate [m³/s]
    • Converted from operational capacity metrics [bpd or m³/day]
  • A: Cross-sectional pipe internal flow area [m²]
  • d: Internal pipe diameter [m]

Mass balance calculations for internal plant operations omit unpublished inlet rates and stream split ratios at the Abqaiq processing hub, as these metrics rely on proprietary estimates rather than open-source records. Primary infrastructure geometries and attribute tables originate from the Global Energy Monitor (GEM) Global Oil Infrastructure Tracker (GOIT) release (`GEM-GOIT-Oil-NGL-Pipelines-2026-06.xlsx` and `GEM-GOIT-Oil-NGL-Pipelines-2026-07-21.gpkg`), distributed under Creative Commons CC BY 4.0, rendered over a standard Google Maps roadmap basemap.
